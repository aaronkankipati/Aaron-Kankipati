Live Market Engine

Neon Bull

I gave an AI ₹10,000 and full control to trade the Indian stock market.

Neon Bull exists because of Project Zeon. I was building an AI trading system connected to a live brokerage account with real money. The APIs were integrated, the logic was in place, and everything was ready to go. But before handing an AI full control over real capital, I needed to know if the engine actually worked. So I built this first. Same system. Same logic. Same market. The only difference is the money is not real yet.

View Live Experiment →

How I Approached It

  • A controlled version: Seeded with ₹10,000 test money, Claude trades autonomously. Stocks, prices, and brokerage charges are real.

  • How it runs: 2 Cloudflare crons fire at 9:30 AM & 1:30 PM IST. Pulls live Yahoo Finance NSE prices, feeds RSI/SMA to Claude Sonnet. No server. No manual trigger.

  • Guardrails: Auto-pauses if portfolio drops below starting capital. Stock names hidden publicly for SEBI compliance.

Product Thinking: Cost Optimization

V1: ₹41 / day

₹0.40 / day

99% Reduction

V4 Evolution: RSI, SMA, and Volume ratio run in pure JS at zero cost. News is parsed via RSS in 200ms. Claude only sees the 0-4 instruments that pass all conditions. Cost dropped not by cutting intelligence, but by moving computation to where it belongs. Zero overrides.

Strategic Pause

Giving an AI complete control over a live brokerage account on day one is interesting. It is also risky. I had the keys, the integrations, and the technical setup ready to go.

But I wanted to prove the engine works before it touches real money. That pause was a product decision, not a fear of shipping.

Live Product

Moodfilm

I am not a developer by profession. I am a Product guy. I built this anyway.

An AI film curator that starts with a simple question: how are you feeling right now? Tell it your mood, pick a language (English, Hindi, or Telugu) and it finds a film matched to that emotional moment, not your watch history. Product thinking over code.

Product Thinking: Infrastructure

Match the model to the task: Mood analysis is pattern recognition—not deep reasoning. Call 1 was switched to Claude Haiku, cutting token ceiling from 900 to 400. Call 2 stayed on Sonnet for reasoning quality, but trimmed to 1,400 tokens.

Cut the input, not the quality: Candidate pool dropped from 10 to 6 films. Overviews trimmed to 80 chars.
